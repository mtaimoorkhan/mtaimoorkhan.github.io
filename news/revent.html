Call for Speakers

Sustainable Cyber Security of Modern Computing Systems and Critical Infrastructures

Modern computing systems and critical infrastructures increasingly combine software, networks, artificial intelligence, cloud and edge services, operational technology, connected devices and physical processes. These include cyber-physical systems, IoT and Industrial IoT, digital twins, autonomous and robotic systems, smart grids, connected transport, healthcare technologies, manufacturing, telecommunications and water systems.

This event will examine the central question: How can confidence in sustainable cyber security be established, measured, communicated and maintained throughout the lifecycle of modern computing systems and critical infrastructures?

Confidence in sustainable cyber security requires credible evidence that security controls remain effective, resilient, maintainable and proportionate over time, while also considering their computational, environmental, economic, operational and societal impacts. The event will explore how assurance, testing, monitoring, risk management, resilience, artificial intelligence, formal modelling and quantum-ready approaches can support this confidence at design time and runtime.

Foundations and Measurement of Confidence

  • Definitions and taxonomies of confidence in sustainable cyber security
  • Security claims, evidence and structured assurance arguments
  • Qualitative, quantitative, probabilistic and risk-based confidence measures
  • Confidence under uncertainty, incomplete evidence and evolving threats
  • Metrics linking security effectiveness with energy, carbon, cost and resource use

Sustainability by Design and Model-Driven Confidence

  • Sustainable-secure-by-design architectures for modern computing systems
  • Formal models, assurance cases and model-based security engineering
  • Security testing, verification, digital twins and cyber-physical modelling
  • Safety-security-sustainability co-engineering and lifecycle assurance
  • Resilience-by-design, graceful degradation, recovery and responsible technology renewal

Sustainability through AI-Driven Monitoring and Runtime Confidence

  • AI-supported threat detection, vulnerability analysis and cyber-risk assessment
  • Machine learning for anomaly detection, intrusion detection and runtime assurance
  • Explainable, trustworthy and accountable AI for cyber-security decisions
  • Adaptive, risk-aware and resource-efficient monitoring of security and environmental costs
  • Predictive resilience, adaptive response and confidence under changing threats

Quantum-Ready Sustainable Cyber Security

  • Cyber-security implications of quantum computing for long-lived systems
  • Post-quantum cryptography and migration to quantum-resistant security
  • Crypto-agility, quantum-safe identity, authentication and key management
  • Assurance and testing of post-quantum and hybrid classical-quantum architectures
  • Standards, governance and organisational roadmaps for quantum readiness

Critical-Infrastructure Security, Resilience and Sustainability

  • Protection of energy, transport, water, healthcare, telecommunications and manufacturing systems
  • IT-OT convergence, cyber-physical attack propagation and cascading disruption
  • Continuity of essential services, adaptive response, recovery and restoration
  • Secure and sustainable infrastructure supply chains and long-lived systems
  • Governance, regulation, compliance, residual risk and public confidence

Cross-Cutting Themes

  • Continuous assurance and dynamic security confidence
  • Sustainability by design and sustainability through monitoring
  • Integration of model-driven, AI-driven, operational and quantum-ready evidence
  • Balancing security, safety, resilience, performance, cost and environmental impact
  • Standards, regulation, human factors, transparency and public trust

Speakers and Contributions

We welcome speakers from academia, industry, government, critical-infrastructure operations, regulation, compliance, engineering, artificial intelligence, quantum technologies, law, risk and assurance, standards and certification, and related professional communities.

Suitable contributions include:

  • Research presentations and position papers
  • Industrial and operational case studies
  • Technical demonstrations, tools, digital twins and testbeds
  • Government, policy and regulatory perspectives
  • Legal, compliance, audit and assurance analyses
  • Incident lessons, resilience practices and recovery experience
  • Panel contributions and strategic challenge statements
  • Proposals for collaborative projects or special interest groups

Speaker proposals should explain:

  • The research, operational or policy problem being addressed
  • The relevant modern computing system or critical-infrastructure context
  • The meaning of sustainable cyber security within the contribution
  • The approach used to establish, measure or maintain confidence
  • The technical, operational or organisational evidence considered
  • The principal findings, limitations and practical lessons
  • The implications for research, industry, governance, regulation or practice
  • Opportunities for future collaboration, deployment or standardisation

Speaker Submission Requirements

  • Presentation title
  • Abstract of approximately 250-300 words
  • Name, role and institutional affiliation
  • Short biography of approximately 100 words
  • Preferred contribution format
  • Relevant sector or disciplinary perspective
  • Accessibility or technical requirements
